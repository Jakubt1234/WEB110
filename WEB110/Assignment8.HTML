Internet Fundamentals: Security

Name:

This Wikiversity lesson, part of the Internet Fundamentals course, introduces Internet security, including browser and browsing security, personally identifiable information (PII), and the ethical and legal issues that come with working in IT.

The first objective is identifying security issues related to Internet clients such as web browsers, email, and instant messaging, especially in the workplace. Key threats include malware, viruses, illicit servers, and problems with digital certificates. Certificates matter because they let a browser verify that a website is genuine and that the connection is encrypted. Email and messaging are common entry points for phishing and malicious attachments, so user awareness is just as important as technical defenses.

The second objective is understanding and applying the principles of Personal Information Management (PIM), including common applications. PIM covers how people organize, store, and protect their own information, such as contacts, calendars, passwords, and files. Closely related is PII, meaning any data that can identify a specific individual, like names, addresses, or financial details. Protecting this kind of information is central to preventing identity theft and fraud.

The third objective is recognizing the ethical and legal issues faced by IT professionals. Because they often have privileged access to sensitive systems and data, they are expected to follow professional codes of ethics, respect privacy, and comply with the law.

The lesson's resources support these goals. The readings include Wikipedia articles on Internet security and personally identifiable information, along with a Wikibooks chapter on the Professional Code of Ethics for IT professionals. The multimedia section lists YouTube videos on encryption and public keys, symmetric versus public key encryption, cybersecurity and crime, and Cybersecurity 101. There is also a student presentation on browser security.

Overall, the lesson builds a foundation in recognizing technical threats, protecting personal and organizational information, and acting responsibly as a technology professional, with encryption concepts underpinning much of how secure communication and trust work online.
